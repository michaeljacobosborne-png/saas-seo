Between July and September 2026, Reddit's share of ChatGPT citations fell 94%.
Over the same quarter its share of Perplexity citations rose 86%.

Both numbers describe the same site in the same period. Average them and you get
something that happened to nobody.

## The measurement published by a monitoring vendor

The figures come from Otterly.AI, published on 1 October 2026 and written by
Rick Tousseyn. Otterly sells AI search visibility monitoring, which makes the
finding more interesting rather than less, because it undercuts the simplest
version of what the category sells.

Their per-platform figures, reproduced here with attribution, cover three
periods: 3 July to 7 August, then 8 to 31 August, then September. Each number is
Reddit's share of that engine's citations.

- **Google AI Overviews**: 4.375%, then 4.490%, then 5.081%. Up 16% over the quarter.
- **Google AI Mode**: 3.625%, then 2.803%, then 4.502%. Up 24%.
- **Perplexity**: 1.176%, then 2.163%, then 2.186%. Up 86%.
- **Gemini**: 1.727%, then 1.220%, then 1.167%. Down 32%.
- **ChatGPT**: 3.765%, then 0.374%, then 0.227%. Down 94%.
- **Microsoft Copilot**: 0.000% in all three periods. No change.
- **Claude**: 0.000% in all three periods. No change.

Three engines moved up. Two moved down. Two never cited Reddit at any point in
the quarter, at three significant figures.

Otterly reports the sharpest move as a single-day event. On 8 August 2026,
reddit.com fell from 4.01% to 0.44% of all ChatGPT citations in the United
States, a decline of 89%, which they attribute to a ChatGPT 5.6 rollout.

### The two checks that make this more than noise

Volume is the obvious alternative explanation, and Otterly closed it off. Over
the same period ChatGPT increased its total daily citations by 23%. ChatGPT did
not stop citing. It stopped citing Reddit.

Concentration is the other. Reddit declined across all 17 industries Otterly
tracks, by between 58% and 99%. No single sector carried the result.

So the decline was not a collection gap and it was not one vertical. Something
changed inside one engine, and Reddit had no input into it.

## Semrush found the same shape a year earlier, on pages that never changed

This is the part worth holding onto, because the Reddit case could be dismissed
as a platform-specific policy move. Semrush's experiment removes that excuse.

In December 2025 Semrush published a 30-day test by Tushar Pol. They put 81 new
FAQ-style pages on their own blog, a domain with an Authority Score of 84, and
tracked citations daily. Best-case conditions, by their own description.

In Google AI Mode, 29 of the 81 pages were cited the day after publication. By
day six, 48 pages. By day 14, 39. By day 30, 21.

Nobody edited those pages. The peak and the trough are the same content, four
weeks apart, measured by the same tool. Semrush's own conclusion: "What gets
cited one week might not be cited the next."

ChatGPT behaved in the opposite direction over the same window, climbing from
eight pages to 34 and holding them. Two engines, one set of pages, opposite
trajectories.

### What two studies together actually establish

Taken alone, each is a single finding from a vendor with a product to sell. Taken
together they establish something harder to argue with.

Citation share moves without any action by the site. It moves by large
percentages over weeks. And it moves in different directions on different engines
at the same time, which is the part that breaks the arithmetic.

## Why this makes a blended score untrue rather than imprecise

An average is only meaningful when the things being averaged are commensurable.
Here they are not, for three separate reasons.

The engines disagree about direction. A quarter that is catastrophic on ChatGPT
and excellent on Perplexity has no honest single summary. Any weighting you pick
is an editorial judgement about which engine matters, presented as a measurement.

Some engines are structurally absent. Copilot and Claude cited Reddit at 0.000%
through the whole quarter. Including a zero in an average implies the zero is a
result. For a site those engines do not use as a source, it is not a result, it
is a category error. Averaging it in drags the number down and makes it look like
a problem you could fix.

The denominators are not stable. These are percentages of each engine's total
citations, and that total is itself moving. ChatGPT's daily citations rose 23%
while Reddit's share of them collapsed. Share and volume can move opposite ways,
so a share figure alone does not tell you whether you were cited more or less
often in absolute terms.

A score that hides all three is not a rounded version of the truth. It is a
different claim.

## Where Otterly's numbers need handling with care

Using this research properly means naming its limits. Four are worth stating.

Otterly is a vendor publishing research that supports buying monitoring from a
vendor. That does not make the data wrong. It does mean the framing is not
neutral, and the same applies to Semrush.

The article gives two different pairs of numbers for what reads as the same
event. The headline is 4.01% falling to 0.44%. The platform table shows ChatGPT
at 3.765% falling to 0.374%. The first is described as US daily share, the second
as period averages, so they are probably different cuts rather than a
contradiction. The article does not reconcile them, so cite whichever you use
with its own scope attached.

The sample is not published on that page. There is no prompt count, query set or
geographic split alongside the table. Otterly documents its methodology
separately and runs a public industry index, so the material exists, but it is
not in the piece carrying the figures.

The comparison periods are unequal, at 36 days, 24 days and 30 days. For share
figures that matters less than it would for counts, but it is not a clean
quarter-on-quarter read.

And the causal claim is a date correlation. A drop coinciding with a model
rollout is consistent with the rollout causing it. It is not a demonstration,
and only OpenAI could supply one.

Separately, Otterly has published a study putting 19.3% of AI search sources at
dead citations. That figure is theirs and is referenced here rather than verified
independently, so treat it as pointing somewhere worth looking rather than as a
settled number.

Reddit is also an unusual subject. It is one of the most heavily cited domains on
the web and the object of commercial negotiation between platforms. A move of
this size on a site like that does not automatically predict what happens to a
mid-size brand.

## What to measure instead

The practical conclusion is not that measurement is hopeless. It is that the unit
has to match what moves.

Report per engine, never blended. If a stakeholder wants one number, give them
the engine that matters to the business and say why that one.

Exclude engines that do not use you as a source, and state the exclusion. A
0.000% that has held for a quarter is a coverage fact, not a performance
figure.

Carry volume next to share. Share can fall while absolute citations rise. Without
both, a line on a chart is ambiguous.

Attach a cause or label it unexplained. The 8 August step coincides with a model
release. Most movement will not have anything to point at, and writing
"unexplained" is more useful than inventing a reason.

Sample enough to separate movement from noise before reporting either. A single
daily reading cannot tell a trend from churn.

### Separate what you control from what you do not

None of the volatility above was caused by anything a site owner did, and none of
it was fixable by anything a site owner could do. That is the real lesson, and it
has a practical edge.

Two things stay under your control. Whether an engine can reach, parse and lift
an answer out of your page. And whether anything in that answer points back to
you, through a named author, a resolvable entity, your own data, or a term that
could only have come from you.

That is the distinction between retrievable and citable, and it is measurable
directly on the page, today, repeatably, without asking an engine anything. It
does not tell you whether you were cited. It tells you whether you are in a
condition to be, which is the part that responds to work.

Measure citation share too, by all means. Just do it per engine, with volume
beside it, and stop pretending a weighted average of seven disagreeing systems is
a fact about your site.

:::faq
### Is AI visibility tracking worth paying for?
It depends what you ask of it. As a per-engine trend over a long enough window,
with a stable prompt set, it answers a real question. As a single blended score
reported weekly, the evidence above says the number will move for reasons
unconnected to anything you did.

### Why do different AI visibility tools give me different numbers?
Different prompt sets, sampling frequencies, geographies and engine coverage.
Otterly's and Semrush's data both show large week-to-week movement in the
underlying citations, so two tools sampling at different times can disagree
without either being wrong.

### If citations are this volatile, what should I optimise for?
The conditions rather than the outcome. Access, structure, extractable answers,
named authorship, resolvable entity markup and original evidence are all
assessable on the page and stable over time. Citation share is downstream of
model behaviour you do not control.

### Does a 0% citation share on an engine mean I have a problem?
Not necessarily. Some engines do not use some source types at all. Reddit held
0.000% on Claude and Copilot for an entire quarter while being one of ChatGPT's
larger sources. Check whether the engine uses sources like yours before treating
a zero as a failure.
:::
